## A Systems View of Project Management

### Core Principle

- **Projects must operate in a broad organizational environment**
- **Project managers need to use systems thinking**: Taking a holistic view of carrying out projects within the context of the organization

### What Is a Systems Approach?

#### Historical Context

- **Systems approach emerged in the 1950s** to describe a holistic and analytical approach to management and problem solving

#### Three Components of Systems Approach

1. **Systems Philosophy**: An overall model for thinking about things as systems
2. **Systems Analysis**: Problem-solving approach
3. **Systems Management**: Address business, technological, and organizational issues before making changes to systems

### The Three-Sphere Model for Systems Management

- **Business Sphere**: Business objectives and requirements
- **Technology Sphere**: Technical capabilities and constraints
- **Organization Sphere**: People, processes, and organizational factors

_Note: Successful projects require balanced consideration of all three spheres_

## Understanding Organizations

### Importance for Project Success

- **Systems approach requires** that project managers always view their projects in the context of the larger organization
- **Organizational issues are often the most difficult part** of working on and managing projects
- Important for project managers to develop a better understanding of **people as well as organizations**
- Critical **to improve the success rate of IT projects**

### The Four Frames of Organizations (Bolman and Deal Model)

#### 1. Structural Frame

- **Focus**: Roles and responsibilities, coordination and control
- **Tool**: Organizational charts help describe this frame
- **Key Elements**: Hierarchy, job descriptions, policies, procedures

#### 2. Human Resource Frame

- **Focus**: Providing harmony between needs of organization and needs of people
- **Key Elements**: Employee development, motivation, work-life balance
- **Goal**: Align individual and organizational goals

#### 3. Political Frame

- **Focus**: Coalitions composed of varied individuals and interest groups
- **Key Issues**: Conflict and power dynamics
- **Reality**: Organizations are political arenas with competing interests

#### 4. Symbolic Frame

- **Focus**: Symbols and meanings related to events
- **Key Elements**: Culture, language, traditions, and image
- **Importance**: How people interpret and make sense of organizational life

## Organizational Structures

### Three Basic Organizational Structures

#### 1. Functional Organization

**Definition**: An organization structure in which people are grouped on the basis of specialization/function and the Project Manager has limited authority to assign work and apply resources.

**Advantages**:

- **Unity of command**: Only one "boss" is giving instructions
- **Workers learn from each other** and keep skills sharp
- **Continue to report to the same Functional Manager**
- **Share resources** among multiple small projects

**Disadvantages**:

- **Slow communications** across multiple functions
- **Technical difficulty** in incorporating input from other disciplines
- **Long communication channels** make for slow decision making and slow response to change

#### 2. Projectized Organization

**Definition**: A structure wherein employees, collocated or not, are grouped by activities on a project and the project manager may have complete, or very close to complete, power over the project team.

**Advantages**:

- **Traditional department barriers are reduced**
- **Unity of command**
- **Communication response times are fast**
- **Co-location**: Team members are physically close

**Disadvantages**:

- **Cost of assigning members** to one part-time project
- **Teamwork methods may differ** from those of the organization
- **Teams may fail to communicate lessons learned**
- **Discipline-specific competence may suffer**

#### 3. Matrix Organization

**Definition**: An organization in which the Project Manager or project lead shares responsibility and accountability for the project with Functional Managers.

**Advantages**:

- **Shared resources** between departments and projects
- **Reduced duplication**
- **Cooperation between departments**
- **High-quality decisions** are well received
- **Continued development** of discipline specific knowledge
- **Effective integration**
- **Lessons learned shared effectively**
- **Flexibility**: weak, balanced, or strong matrix

**Disadvantages**:

- **Each employee has two "bosses"**
- **More sources of conflict**
- **More meetings**
- **More challenges to control**

### Matrix Organization Variations

|Organizational Question|Functional|Weak Matrix|Balanced Matrix|Strong Matrix|Projectized|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Who has power?|FM almost all power|FM more|Equally shared|PM more|PM almost all power|

### 360-Degree Performance Reviews

- **Process**: Functional manager performs evaluation by seeking feedback from representative sample of staff who have worked with the project team member
- **Best Practice**: Being appraised by peers or team members who observed the individual "in the trenches"
- **Usage**: Many large organizations use this technique, especially when staff rarely see their direct supervisor
- **Benefit**: More comprehensive and realistic performance assessment

## Organizational Culture and Its Impact on Projects

### Definition

**Organizational culture** is a set of shared assumptions, values, and behaviors that characterize the functioning of an organization

### Expert Perspective

- Many experts believe the **underlying causes of many companies' problems are not the structure or staff, but the culture**
- **"Values serve as a moral compass to guide us and provide a frame of reference to set priorities and determine right or wrong."** - Craig Johnson

### Two Important Aspects

#### 1. Culture of the Parent Organization

**Key Questions to Consider**:

- What is the corporate culture in general?
- What are the ascribed values?
- Are there standard project management practices and policies?
- How is the organization viewed by others in terms of living the values?
- How does the organization communicate?
- How does the organization support project management?

**Critical Point**: A successful Project Manager needs to understand the organizational culture.

#### 2. Project Cultural Norms

**Core Cultural Norms**:

|Norm|Description|
|---|---|
|**Responsibility**|Own decisions|
|**Respect**|Ourselves, others, resources|
|**Fairness**|Impartial|
|**Honesty**|Truth|

**Influences**:

- **Impacted by parent organization's culture**
- **Influenced by sponsor and Project Manager**
- **An ethical culture both defines how people should act and encourages them to do so**—especially in challenging circumstances

### Cultural Impact Areas

- **Motivate ethical actions and communications**
- **Determine how people are treated, controlled, and rewarded**
- **Establish how cooperation, competition, conflict, and decision making are handled**
- **Encourage personal commitment to the organization**

### Types of Organizational Power Culture

|Culture Type|Description|
|---|---|
|**Power**|Everyone tries to please "the boss"|
|**Role**|Everyone follows designated roles|
|**Task**|Getting the job done is most important|
|**Personal**|Interest in worker development/needs|

## Focusing on Stakeholder Needs

### Project Manager Responsibilities

- **Project managers must take time to identify, understand, and manage relationships** with all project stakeholders
- **Using the four frames of organizations** can help meet stakeholder needs and expectations
- **Senior executives/top management/sponsor** are very important stakeholders

### Common Causes of Project Success and Failure

#### Major Failure Factors

- **Incomplete or unclear requirements**
- **Inadequate user involvement**
- **Inadequate resources**
- **Unrealistic time demands**
- **Unclear or unrealistic expectations**
- **Inadequate executive support**
- **Changing requirements**
- **Inadequate planning**

### Plan-Driven Project Roles

#### Executive Roles

- **Sponsor**: Provides overall direction and funding
- **Customer**: End user or beneficiary of project outcomes
- **Steering Team**: Senior management oversight group

#### Managerial Roles

- **Project Manager**: Day-to-day project leadership and coordination
- **Functional Manager**: Manages resources from specific departments
- **Project Management Office**: Provides standards and support

#### Associate Roles

- **Core Team Member**: Key project contributors
- **Subject Matter Expert (SME)**: Specialized knowledge providers
- **Facilitator**: Process and meeting management support

### Project Decision-Making Guide

|Person/Method|When to Use|
|---|---|
|**Sponsor decides**|Critical decision, large monetary stake, "big picture" needed|
|**Project manager decides**|Time is critical, no need for other input|
|**Functional manager decides**|'How' functional work is done|
|**Core team discusses and Project Manager decides**|Team input is useful|
|**Core team consensus**|Buy-in is critical|
|**Delegated to one or two team members to recommend**|Needs to be investigated, team input useful|
|**Delegated to one or two team members to decide**|Needs to be investigated, team input not needed|

## The Importance of Top Management Commitment

### Key Stakeholder Role

- **People in top management positions are key stakeholders** in projects
- **A very important factor** in helping project managers successfully lead projects is the **level of commitment and support they receive from top management**
- **Without top management commitment, many projects will fail**

### Project Champions

- Some projects have a **senior manager called a champion** who acts as a key proponent for a project
- Champions provide advocacy and support at executive level

### How Top Management Can Help Project Managers

- **Providing adequate resources**
- **Approving unique project needs in a timely manner**
- **Getting cooperation from other parts of the organization**
- **Mentoring and coaching on leadership issues**

## The Need for Organizational Standards

### Benefits of Standards

- **Standards and guidelines help project managers be more effective**

### Senior Management Support Areas

- **Use of standard forms and software** for project management
- **Development and use of guidelines** for writing project plans or providing status information
- **Creation of a project management office or center of excellence**

## Project and Product Life Cycles

### General Principle

- **It is good practice to divide projects into several phases**
- **Because projects operate as part of a system and involve uncertainty**
- **The same can be said for developing products**

### Project Life Cycle

#### Definition

**A project life cycle is a collection of project phases that defines**:

- **What work will be performed** in each phase
- **What deliverables will be produced and when**
- **Who is involved** in each phase
- **How management will control and approve** work produced in each phase

**Key Term**: A **deliverable** is a product or service produced or provided as part of a project

#### Four Main Project Life Cycle Phases

1. **Selecting and initiating**
2. **Planning**
3. **Executing** (includes monitoring/controlling)
4. **Closing and realizing**

#### Phase Characteristics

##### Early Phases

- **Resource needs are usually lowest**
- **Level of uncertainty (risk) is highest**
- **Project stakeholders have the greatest opportunity to influence the project**

##### Middle Phases

- **Certainty of completing a project improves**
- **More resources are needed**

##### Final Phase

- **Focus on ensuring that project requirements were met**
- **Sponsor approves completion of the project**

### Product Life Cycles

#### Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

**Definition**: A framework for describing the phases of developing information systems

#### Types of Development Life Cycles

1. **Predictive life cycle**: Traditional sequential approach
2. **Iterative life cycle**: Repeated cycles of development
3. **Incremental life cycle**: Building in functional increments
4. **Adaptive life cycle**: Flexible, change-driven approach
5. **Hybrid life cycle**: Combination of approaches

#### Predictive Life Cycle Models

##### Waterfall Model

- **Has well-defined, linear stages** of systems development and support
- Sequential approach with distinct phases

##### Spiral Model

- **Shows that software is developed using an iterative or spiral approach** rather than a linear approach
- Risk-driven development process

##### Prototyping Model

- **Used for developing prototypes** to clarify user requirements
- Early working models to validate concepts

##### Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model

- **Used to produce systems quickly** without sacrificing quality
- Fast development through tools and techniques

### The Importance of Project Phases and Management Reviews

#### Phase Gates

- **A project should successfully pass through each of the project phases** in order to continue on to the next
- **Management reviews** (also called **phase exits**, **phase gate reviews**, or **kill points**) should occur after each phase

#### Purpose of Management Reviews

- **Evaluate the project's progress**
- **Assess likely success**
- **Determine continued compatibility with organizational goals**

#### Best Practice

- **It is unwise to wait until the end of project or product phases** to have management inputs
- **Many projects are reviewed by management on a regular basis**

## Recent Trends Affecting Information Technology Project Management

### Three Major Trends

#### 1. Globalization

**Definition**: Globalization enables and accelerates global integration—connecting people, systems, and businesses across borders through networks, data, and digital tools. It's both a driver and a result of globalization.

**Issues**:

- **Communications** challenges across time zones and cultures
- **Trust** building in distributed teams
- **Common work practices** standardization
- **Tools** compatibility and access

**Suggestions for Managing Global Projects**:

- **Employ greater project discipline**
- **Think globally but act locally**
- **Consider collaboration over standardization**
- **Keep project momentum going**
- **Use newer tools and technology**

#### 2. Outsourcing

**Definition**: Outsourcing is when an organization acquires goods and/or sources from an outside source. **Offshoring** is sometimes used to describe outsourcing from another country.

**Key Points**:

- **Organizations remain competitive** by using outsourcing to their advantage, such as finding ways to reduce costs
- **Practice can be unpopular in some countries**
- **Project managers should become more familiar** with many global and procurement issues

#### 3. Virtual Teams

**Definition**: A virtual team is a group of individuals who work across time and space using communication technologies.

**Advantages**:

- **Lowering costs** because many virtual workers do not require office space or support beyond their home offices
- **Providing more expertise and flexibility** or increasing competitiveness and responsiveness by having team members from across the globe working any time of day or night
- **Improving the work/life balance** for team members by eliminating fixed office hours and the need to travel to work

**Disadvantages**:

- **Isolating team members**
- **Increasing the potential for communications problems**
- **Reducing the ability for team members to network** and transfer information informally
- **Increasing the dependence on technology** to accomplish work

## Chapter Summary - Key Takeaways

### Systems Thinking

- **Project managers need to take a systems approach** when working on projects
- Must consider business, technology, and organizational factors simultaneously

### Organizational Understanding

- **Organizations have four different frames**: structural, human resources, political, and symbolic
- **The structure and culture of an organization have strong implications** for project managers
- Understanding organizational context is critical for project success

### Project Management Process

- **Projects should successfully pass through each phase** of the project life cycle
- Regular management reviews and phase gates are essential
- **Project managers need to consider several factors** due to the unique context of information technology projects

### Contemporary Challenges

- **Recent trends affecting IT project management** include globalization, outsourcing, and virtual teams
- These trends require new skills and approaches from project managers
- Technology enables new ways of working but also creates new challenges
