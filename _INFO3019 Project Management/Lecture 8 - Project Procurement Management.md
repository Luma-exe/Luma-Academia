## What is Project Procurement Management

### Definition

Project procurement management involves **acquiring goods and services for a project from outside the performing organization**.

**Formal Definition:** "A system's approach to managing all the flows of physical products, information, and funds from suppliers and producers, through resellers or intermediaries, and finally through the project organization for creating customer satisfaction."

### Key Roles in Procurement

#### Owner

- Person or entity that owns the product of the project
- Product will be handed over to them at project completion

#### General Contractor

- Person or agency that does not specialize in one kind of work
- Often refers to the primary contractor who employs specialty subcontractors

#### Subcontractor

- **First-tier subcontractor:** Holds contract with prime contractor
- **Lower-tier subcontractor:** Holds contract with a subcontractor to the prime contractor

## Project Procurement Management Processes

### Three Main Processes

#### Planning Procurement Management

**Purpose:** Determining what to procure and when and how to do it

- Identifying which project needs can best be met by using products or services outside the organization
- Involves deciding whether to procure, how to procure, what to procure, how much to procure, and when to procure
- **Key Output:** Make-or-buy decision
- **Important Note:** If there is no need to buy any products or services from outside the organization, then there is no need to perform any of the other procurement management processes

#### Conducting Procurements

**Purpose:** Obtaining seller responses, selecting sellers, and awarding contracts

- Steps include:
    - Decide whom to ask to do the work
    - Send appropriate documentation to potential sellers
    - Obtain proposals or bids
    - Select a seller
    - Award a contract

#### Controlling Procurements

**Purpose:** Managing relationships with sellers, monitoring contract performance, making changes as needed, and closing out contracts

## Planning Procurement Management

### Project Procurement Components

#### Make-or-Buy Decisions

Choosing between manufacturing a product or developing a service in-house versus purchasing it from an external supplier.

#### Contract Types

Compare advantages and disadvantages when a buy decision is warranted.

#### Collaboration and Cooperation

Coordinate project activities to ensure deliverables are produced as scheduled.

#### System Integration

Consider trade-offs among project goals such as time, cost, and scope (including quality).

### Prerequisites for Planning

- Complete most of the project planning first
- **Minimum requirement:** Project scope statement

### Make-or-Buy Decision Analysis

|**Reasons to Make**|**Reasons to Buy**|
|---|---|
|Lower production cost|Frees project team to deal with other important activities|
|More control over quality and time|Ability to utilize specialized suppliers|
|Lack of suitable suppliers|Flexibility in procurement|
|Obtain a customized item|Inadequate managerial or technical resources|
|Utilize project team's expertise and time|Inadequate capacity|
|Protect proprietary design or knowledge|Small volume requirements|
||Ability to procure work elements that are complex or high-tech|

### Key Planning Outputs

#### Procurement Management Plan

- Portion of the project management plan that describes how a project team will acquire goods and services they choose to purchase
- Describes how the procurement processes will be managed, from developing documentation for making outside purchases or acquisitions to contract closure
- Contents vary based on project needs

#### Procurement Statement of Work (SOW)

Documents the portion of work to be purchased, described in enough detail so potential suppliers can decide if they are capable of and interested in providing it.

**Contract Statement of Work:** When SOW is used as part of a contract to describe only the work required for that particular contract (a type of scope statement).

**Purpose:** A good SOW gives bidders a better understanding of the buyer's expectations.

## Project Procurement Request Types

### Request for Proposal (RFP)

- Used at early stage in procurement process
- Issues invitation for suppliers to submit a proposal on a specific commodity or service
- Often through a bidding process

### Request for Information (RFI)

- Proposal requested from potential seller or service provider
- Determines what products and services are potentially available in the marketplace
- Assesses capability of seller in terms of offerings and strengths

### Request for Quotation (RFQ)

- Used when discussions with bidders are not required
- Mainly when specifications of a product or service are already known
- Price is the main or only factor in selecting the successful bidder

## Types of Contracts

### Fixed-Price Contracts

#### Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) Contract

- Seller must complete the job for the agreed-upon amount of money regardless of actual cost incurred
- **Risk:** Absorbed by seller
- **Appropriate when:** Costs are well known

#### Firm-Fixed-Incentive-Fee (FPIF) Contract

- Price is fixed as defined by the contract
- Seller can earn additional amount as incentive if meeting defined project metrics
- **Risk:** Mostly absorbed by seller
- **Appropriate when:** Costs are well known and buyer wants to maximize some performance aspects

#### Firm-Fixed-Economic-Price-Adjustment (FP-EPA) Contract

- Fixed-price contract with clause to protect seller from conditions such as inflation or commodity cost increases
- **Risk:** Absorbed by both parties
- **Appropriate when:** Project may be of long duration, and inflation and commodity prices may fluctuate

**Point of Total Assumption (PTA):** Cost at which the contractor assumes total responsibility for each additional dollar of contract cost.

### Cost-Reimbursable Contracts

Involve payment to the seller for direct and indirect costs.

#### Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF) Contract

- Buyer reimburses seller for all allowable costs plus a fixed amount of profit (fee)
- **Risk:** Absorbed by buyer
- **Appropriate when:** Costs not well known

#### Cost-Plus-Award-Fee (CPAF) Contract

- Payments to seller for all allowed costs incurred for completed work
- Plus an award fee based on satisfying certain subjective performance objectives
- **Risk:** Mostly absorbed by buyer
- **Appropriate when:** Both parties agree most of the award fee is based upon buyer's opinion of seller performance on stated criteria

#### Cost-Plus-Incentive-Fee (CPIF) Contract

- Buyer reimburses seller for allowable costs
- Pays seller a fee if it meets defined performance criteria such as schedule, cost, and/or performance
- **Risk:** Mostly absorbed by buyer
- **Appropriate when:** Costs are not well known, and buyer wants to maximize some performance aspects

### Time and Material (T&M) Contracts

**Hybrid contracts** containing aspects of both cost-reimbursement and fixed-price contracts.

**Characteristics:**

- Generally used when the deliverable is labor hours and/or amounts of materials
- Unit rate for each hour of labor or pound of material is set in the contract
- Amount of work is not set → the value of the contract can grow
- Seller charges for what is done to produce the product/service in the contract
- **Risk:** Absorbed by buyer
- **Appropriate when:** Cost rates known, volumes are unknown

### Unit Price Contracts

Require the buyer to pay the seller a predetermined amount per unit of service.

### Contract Risk Summary Table

|**Contract Type**|**Cost Risk Absorbed By**|**Appropriate When**|
|---|---|---|
|Firm-fixed-price|Seller|Costs are well known|
|Fixed-price-incentive-fee|Mostly seller|Costs are well known and buyer wants to maximize some performance aspects|
|Fixed-price-economic-price-adjustment|Both|Project may be of long duration, and inflation and commodity prices may fluctuate|
|Cost-plus-incentive-fee|Mostly buyer|Costs are not well known, and buyer wants to maximize some performance aspects|
|Cost-plus-award-fee|Mostly buyer|Both parties agree most of the award fee is based upon buyer's opinion of seller performance on stated criteria|
|Cost-plus-fixed-fee|Buyer|Costs not well known|
|Time and material|Buyer|Cost rates known, volumes are unknown|

### Important Contract Considerations

- Contracts should include specific clauses to take into account issues unique to the project
- Time and material contracts and unit-price contracts can be high or low risk, depending on the nature of the project and other contract clauses
- **Termination clause:** Allows the buyer or supplier to end the contract

## Controlling Procurements

### Primary Purpose

Ensures the seller's performance meets contractual requirements.

### Key Considerations

- **Legal Nature:** Contracts are legal relationships, so it is important that legal and contracting professionals be involved in writing and administering contracts
- **Team Awareness:** Project team members must be aware of potential legal problems they might cause by not understanding a contract
- **Constructive Change Orders:** Project managers and team members must watch for these
    - **Definition:** Oral or written acts or omissions by someone with actual or apparent authority that can be construed to have the same effect as a written change order

### Change Control Suggestions for Contracts

#### Change Review Process

- Changes to any part of the project need to be reviewed, approved, and documented by the same people in the same way that the original part of the plan was approved

#### Impact Analysis

- Evaluation of any change should include an impact analysis

#### Documentation Requirements

- Changes must be documented in writing

#### Stakeholder Involvement

- Project managers and teams should stay closely involved to make sure the new system will meet business needs and work in an operational environment

#### Contingency Planning

- Have backup plans

#### Tools and Techniques

- Contract change control system
- Buyer-conducted performance reviews
- Inspections and audits

### Closing Procurements

#### Process Overview

- Involves completing and settling contracts and resolving any open items
- The project team should determine if all work was completed correctly and satisfactorily
- Update records to reflect final results
- Archive information for future use
- **Contract Requirement:** The contract itself should include requirements for formal acceptance and closure

#### Tools for Contract Closure

##### Procurement Audits

Identify lessons learned in the procurement process.

##### Records Management System

Provides the ability to easily organize, find, and archive procurement-related documents.

##### Dispute Resolution

- **Preferred Method:** All procurements should end in a negotiated settlement between the buyer and the seller
- **Alternative Methods:** If negotiation is not possible, then some type of alternate dispute resolution such as mediation or arbitration can be used

##### Information Archiving

Archiving information for future use is particularly important.

## Considerations for Adaptive Environments

### Agile Approach to Procurement

- **Agile Manifesto Value:** Customer collaboration over contract negotiation, setting an important tone for procurement relationships on agile projects
- **Collaborative Approach:** The buyer and seller should work together to create the required products and services throughout the entire procurement process

### Speed Considerations

- **Adaptive Goal:** Another goal of adaptive environments is speed
- **Challenge:** Several procurements take time
- **Process Integrity:** Processes of project procurement management follow a clear, logical sequence
- **Best Practice:** Project managers and their teams must follow good project procurement management

### Comparison: Plan-Driven vs Adaptive Approaches

|**Procurement Questions**|**Plan-Driven**|**Adaptive**|
|---|---|---|
|**How are make or buy decisions made?**|Reasons for both make or buy are considered|Reasons for both make or buy are considered|
|**How are suppliers treated?**|Fairly as vendors|Fairly as partners|
|**How are clients treated?**|Fairly as customers|Fairly as partners|
|**What factors are used in selecting suppliers?**|Technical, managerial, financial, experiential|Primarily alignment with project vision, but also technical, managerial, financial, and experiential|
|**What is emphasized in procurement agreements?**|Cost, schedule, scope, and contract incentives|Value chain, transparency, flexibility, and collaboration|
|**What types of contracts are typically used?**|Fixed price (FFP, FPIF, and FPEPA), Cost plus (CPIF, CPAF, CPFF), and time and material|Change for free, fixed price increment, graduated fixed price, and money for nothing|

## Key Terminology and Definitions

- **Buyer:** May be called a customer, service requestor, or purchaser
- **Seller:** May be called a supplier, supplier's supplier, or contractor
- **Competitive Advantage:** May be defined as lower cost, better quality, and/or fast delivery
- **Procurement Documents:** Documents that define the requirements and contractual relationship between suppliers and customers
