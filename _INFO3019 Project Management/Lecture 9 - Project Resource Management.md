## The Importance of Resource Management

### Key Principles

- **People determine the success and failure of organizations and projects**
- Most project managers agree that managing human resources effectively is one of the toughest challenges they face
- Managing people is a vital component of project resource management

### Current Workforce Considerations

- Global IT workforce dynamics and future implications must be considered
- Resource availability, specifically human resources, is a major constraint for projects

## What is Project Resource Management?

**Definition**: Making the most effective use of the human and physical resources involved with a project

### Core Processes

1. **Planning resource management**
2. **Estimating activity resources**
3. **Acquiring resources**
4. **Developing the project team**
5. **Managing the project team**
6. **Controlling resources**

## Behavioral Skills Needed by Project Managers

### Essential Skills

- Selecting people who work well in teams
- Identifying exactly what each person needs to accomplish (individually and collectively)
- Ensuring that each person has the desire to improve
- Dealing with difficult individual work schedules
- Getting people to work overtime when there are schedule conflicts
- Making honest and open estimates of the amount of work required
- Assembling an effective team
- Dealing with people from diverse backgrounds
- Deciding where each person will work
- Deciding how a geographically dispersed team can work virtually and effectively

## Considerations When Resourcing Projects

### Key Constraints and Trade-offs

- **Projects always have trade-offs** with respect to resources among:
    - Schedule
    - Human resources
    - Costs
    - Quality
    - Scope
- **Resources are limited** - Project Managers must recognize resource limitations to prevent overpromising
- **People often constitute a large portion of the total project cost**
- **Managing people is the most challenging aspect of a project**
- **Resource availability, specifically human resources, is a major constraint for projects**

## Influence and Power

### Ways to Have Influence (Thamhain and Wilemon)

#### Types of Influence

1. **Authority**: Legitimate hierarchical right to issue orders
2. **Assignment**: Ability to influence a worker's later work assignments
3. **Budget**: Ability to authorize others' use of discretionary funds
4. **Promotion**: Ability to improve a worker's position
5. **Money**: Ability to increase a worker's pay and benefits
6. **Penalty**: Ability to cause punishment
7. **Work challenge**: Ability to assign work that capitalizes on a worker's enjoyment of doing a particular task
8. **Expertise**: Perceived special knowledge that others deem important
9. **Friendship**: Ability to establish friendly personal relationships between the project manager and others

#### Effective Influence Strategies

- **Most Effective**: Project managers who use **work challenges and expertise** to influence people - projects are more likely to succeed
- **Least Effective**: Projects are more likely to fail when project managers rely too heavily on **authority, money, or penalty**

### Power vs. Influence

- **Power**: The potential ability to influence behavior to get people to do things they would not otherwise do
- **Power is much stronger than influence** because it is often used to force people to change their behavior

#### Types of Power

1. **Coercive**
2. **Legitimate**
3. **Expert**
4. **Reward**
5. **Referent**

## Plan Resource Management

### Definition

**Plan Resource Management** is the process of identifying resources and required skills for a project, defining and assigning roles and responsibilities to all resources, developing a reporting hierarchy, and communicating expectations.

### Staffing Management Plan

**Definition**: A proposal focused on acquiring, developing, and retaining human resources for as long as you need them on the project.

## Estimating Activity Resources

### Definition

**Estimating Activity Resources** is a process of assessing all types of resources—people, materials, tools, and equipment (along with quantities)—required for each activity to complete it as specified in project scope.

### Tools for Resource Estimating

- **Expert judgment**
- **Various estimating approaches**
- **Data analysis**
- **Project management software**
- **Meetings**

## Resource Assignment

### Key Considerations

- After developing resource requirements, project managers must work with other people in their organizations to assign them to their projects or acquire additional resources
- Organizations that do a good job of staff acquisition have good staffing plans
- Very important to consider the needs of individuals and the organization when making recruiting and retention decisions

### Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM)

**Definition**: A matrix depicting work packages and assigned resources for completing activities required to produce work packages.

### RACI Chart

**RACI** is a popular form of RAM that presents roles of key stakeholders with roles defined as:

- **R**: Responsible
- **A**: Accountable
- **C**: Consult
- **I**: Inform

#### Adaptive Teams

In adaptive environments, team members decide among themselves who will do each work activity; when finished, they begin next-highest-priority work.

## Resource Loading and Leveling

### Resource Loading

**Definition**: Resource loading refers to the number of individual resources an existing schedule requires during specific time periods.

#### Purpose

- Helps project managers develop a general understanding of the demands a project will make on the organization's resources and individual people's schedules
- **Overallocation**: More resources than available are assigned to perform work at a given time

### Methods of Resolving Resource Overloads

1. Reorder activities
2. Acquire or borrow additional resources
3. Reduce project scope or extend project schedule
4. Inform sponsor of severe overloads
5. Resource-level the overloaded person's schedule
6. Assign certain activities to other workers
7. Split an activity into two activities:
    - Perform first part as scheduled
    - Delay second part of activity

### Resource Leveling

**Definition**: A project execution technique of adjusting the use of resources based on availability and the amount of float on activities to accomplish work as soon as possible, given the limited resource availability.

#### Important Notes

- Resource leveling often reduces an overload, but it does not always eliminate it entirely
- If non-critical activities must be completed at the rate of effort shown in the original schedule, some may need to be assigned to another worker
- Resource leveling can be as much art as science

## Schedule Compression Techniques

### Actions to Reduce the Critical Path

1. Reduce project scope and/or quality
2. Overlap sequential activities
3. Partially overlap sequential activities
4. Increase number of work hours/days
5. Schedule activities at same time
6. Shorten activities by assigning more resources
7. Shorten activities that cost least to speed up

### Crashing

**Definition**: Speeding up the critical path often by adding additional resources or employing existing resources for longer hours and/or more days per week.

#### Decision Criteria for Crashing

Two key questions must be asked:

1. **Which activities are on the critical path?**
2. **Which critical path activity costs the least on a per-day basis to speed up?**

#### Agile Equivalent

In Agile, crashing is called **"swarming,"** since team members swarm on a problem to help get back on track.

### Fast Tracking

**Definition**: A method to expedite a project by executing activities at the same time that would ordinarily be done one after the other.

**Process**: Fast tracking occurs when activities that are normally performed in series (one after the other) are performed at the same time.

## Predictive versus Adaptive Project Resourcing

### Comparison Table

|Project Resourcing Questions|Predictive|Adaptive|
|---|---|---|
|Ideally how long is the project team together?|Duration of project|Long-term|
|What is the more likely limiting schedule factor?|Activities and their logical order and estimated durations|Size and ability of the team|
|What is needed to get the team going?|Identify potential resources, decide timing, select, and on-board members|Keep the existing team going|
|How is work divided among team members?|Project manager assigns then finds and resolves overloads|Team decides who does what and supports each other as needed|
|How is a predictable amount of project work managed?|A worker is assigned to each activity, then overloads are identified and resolved|Teams determine their velocity, then manage their cadence and cycle time|
|How are schedule compression issues handled?|Reduce scope or critical path, often by fast tracking or crashing|Reduce scope by delivering MVP early and letting the client decide|

### Key Differences

- **Traditional projects**: Reduce uncertainty with a primary focus on following the plan
- **Agile projects**: Change-driven, and uncertainty is embraced

## Developing the Project Team

### Main Goal

Help people work together more effectively to improve project performance - teamwork is essential to successfully complete most projects.

### Tuckman Model - Five Stages of Team Development

#### Stage 1: Forming

- **Team member relationship issues**: Feel excitement, yet skepticism
- **Team members attempt to**: Understand expectations, activities needed, and power structures
- **PM strategies for organization**: Develop business case and acceptance criteria in charter
- **PM strategies for project**: Develop scope overview, milestone schedule, risks, and learnings in charter
- **PM strategies for team members**: Develop team operating methods and commitment in charter, help members build relationships

#### Stage 2: Storming

- **Team member relationship issues**: Feel resistance, yet longing to commit to project
- **Team members attempt to**: Jockey for power, ask many questions, and establish dubious goals
- **PM strategies for organization**: Develop stakeholder analysis, communication plan, budget, and quality plan
- **PM strategies for project**: Develop scope statement, Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), schedule, and risk register
- **PM strategies for team members**: Clarify each member's role, encourage all to participate, and determine team ground rules expanding upon operating methods in charter

#### Stage 3: Norming

- **Team member relationship issues**: Feel part of team and believe project will succeed
- **Team members attempt to**: Accept team members, hold open discussions, and establish team norms
- **PM strategies for organization**: Manage trade-offs per stakeholder desires, include sponsor in talks, and conduct audit
- **PM strategies for project**: Add SMEs as needed, authorize work, and improve work processes
- **PM strategies for team members**: Personalize each member's role, collaborate when possible, and assess and build members' and team's capability

#### Stage 4: Performing

- **Team member relationship issues**: Feel close to teammates and understand teammates
- **Team members attempt to**: Improve self, prevent and solve problems, and expand beyond official role
- **PM strategies for organization**: Share applied learnings with organization and report progress to stakeholders
- **PM strategies for project**: Monitor and control project according to plan and update plans as needed
- **PM strategies for team members**: Capture applied learnings and improve meeting and time management

#### Stage 5: Adjourning

- **Team member relationship issues**: Feel strong attachment to team and feel loss when team disbands
- **Team members attempt to**: Complete project on high note, maintain relationships with teammates, and seek next challenge
- **PM strategies for organization**: Secure customer acceptance of deliverables, honestly appraise team members, and provide ongoing support to users
- **PM strategies for project**: Test project deliverables and secure team member endorsement of them
- **PM strategies for team members**: Celebrate success, reward team members, and help team members secure follow-on work

## Managing the Project Team

### Project Manager Responsibilities

- Lead their teams in performing various project activities
- After assessing team performance and related information, make several decisions:
    - Changes to be requested
    - Corrective or preventive actions
    - Updates needed

### Tools and Techniques Available

- **Interpersonal and team skills**
- **Project management information systems**
- **Conflict management**

### Five Dysfunctions of Teams

1. **Absence of trust**
2. **Fear of conflict**
3. **Lack of commitment**
4. **Avoidance of accountability**
5. **Inattention to results**

### General Advice for Managing Teams

- Be patient and kind with your team
- Fix the problem instead of blaming people
- Establish regular, effective meetings
- Allow time for teams to go through the basic team-building stages
- **Limit the size of work teams to three to seven members**
- Plan some social activities to help project team members and other stakeholders get to know each other better
- Stress team identity
- Nurture team members and encourage them to help each other
- Acknowledge individual and group accomplishments
- Take additional actions to work with virtual team members

## Controlling Resources

### Purpose

- Ensuring physical resources assigned to the project are available as planned
- Monitoring the planned versus actual resources utilization
- Taking corrective actions as needed

## Considerations for Agile/Adaptive Environments

### Key Characteristics

- **Teams are important on all types of projects** - collaboration, problem solving, and knowledge sharing are essential
- On agile projects, **team members are usually fully dedicated to a single team**
- **Relationships are based on trust**
- **Collaboration is continuously improved using regular feedback**

## Key Takeaways

### Critical Success Factors

- Use **work challenges and expertise** rather than authority, money, or penalty for influence
- Understand the **Tuckman model** stages and appropriate PM strategies for each
- Know the difference between **crashing** (adding resources) and **fast tracking** (parallel activities)
- Understand **RACI matrix** roles and responsibilities
- Remember that **managing people is the most challenging aspect** of project management

### Important Definitions to Remember

- **Resource Loading**: Number of resources required during specific time periods
- **Resource Leveling**: Adjusting resource use based on availability and float
- **Overallocation**: More resources assigned than available
- **RBS**: Resource Breakdown Structure organizing resources by category
- **RAM**: Responsibility Assignment Matrix showing work packages and assigned resources
