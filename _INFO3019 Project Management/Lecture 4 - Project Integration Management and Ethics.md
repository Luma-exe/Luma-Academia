## Strategic Planning and Project Selection

### Strategic Planning Process
- **Strategic planning** involves determining long-term objectives
- Analyzing the strengths and weaknesses of an organization
- Studying opportunities and threats in the business environment
- Predicting future trends
- Projecting the need for new products and services

### SWOT Analysis
- **SWOT analysis**: Structured planning method used to evaluate the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats involved in a project or in a business venture

#### Internal Factors (Strengths and Weaknesses)
- What does the business do well
- What unique resources do you have
- What could you improve

#### External Factors (Opportunities and Threats)
- What opportunities are open to you
- Are there trends you could take advantage of
- What is your competition doing

### Strategic Objectives
- **Strategic Objectives** are:
  - A means of achieving the vision and mission
  - Objective setting occurs annually
  - Short-term and long-term description of results
  - Descriptions of measures of achievement

#### SMART Objectives
Effective objectives are **SMART**:
- **S**pecific
- **M**easurable
- **A**chievable
- **R**esults-based
- **T**ime-specific

### Methods for Selecting Projects

#### Key Selection Questions
- What value does potential project brings to the organization?
- Are the demands of performing each project understood?
- Are the resources needed to perform the project available?
- Is it feasible to complete the project within the expected time and at the projected cost while managing associated risks?
- Is the project financially beneficial and compatible with other investment decisions?
- Is there enthusiastic support both from external customers and from one or more internal champions?
- Which projects will best help the organization achieve its strategic goals?

#### Financial Analysis Methods

##### Net Present Value (NPV) Analysis
- **Definition**: Method of calculating the expected net monetary gain or loss from a project by discounting all expected future cash inflows and outflows to the present point in time
- Projects with a positive NPV should be considered if financial value is a key criterion
- Projects with higher NPVs are preferred

**NPV Formula**:
```
NPV = Σ [Net Cash Flow t / (1+r)^t]

Where:
- Net Cash Flow = Benefit - Cost for year t
- r = Discount rate (e.g., 8% or 0.08)
- t = Year number
```

**NPV Calculation Steps**:
1. Determine estimated costs and benefits for the life of the project and the products it produces
2. Determine the discount rate
3. Calculate the net present value

**Important Considerations**:
- Some organizations refer to the investment year or years for project costs as Year 0 and do not discount costs in Year 0
- Discount rate can vary, often based on the prime rate and other economic considerations
- Costs can be entered as negative numbers and can be listed first (and then benefits)

##### Return on Investment (ROI)
- **Definition**: Calculated by subtracting the project costs from the benefits and then dividing by the costs

**ROI Formula**:
```
ROI = (Total Benefits - Total Cost) / Total Cost × 100
```

- The higher the ROI, the better
- Many organizations have a required rate of return (minimum acceptable rate of return on investment for projects)
- **Internal rate of return (IRR)** can be calculated by finding the discount rate that makes the NPV equal to zero

##### Payback Analysis
- **Definition**: The amount of time it will take to recoup, in the form of net cash inflows, the total dollars invested in a project
- Determines how much time will elapse before accrued benefits overtake accrued and continuing costs
- Payback occurs when the net cumulative discounted benefits equals the costs
- Many organizations have requirements for the length of the payback period of an investment

#### Weighted Scoring Model

**Process**:
1. Identify criteria important to the project selection process
2. Assign weights (percentages) to each criterion so they add up to 100%
3. Assign scores to each criterion for each project
4. Multiply the scores by the weights and get the total weighted scores

**Benefits**:
- Provides a systematic process for selecting projects based on many criteria
- Incorporates both quantitative and qualitative factors
- Allows for transparent decision-making

## Ethics in Project Management

### PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct
The PMI Code of Ethics provides a framework for ethical decision-making based on four core values:

#### Four Core Values

##### Responsibility
- **Definition**: Owning decisions and actions
- **In Action**:
  - Acknowledge mistakes and take corrective action
  - Follow legal and organizational policies

##### Respect
- **Definition**: Treating all stakeholders fairly and professionally
- **In Action**:
  - Treat all team members and stakeholders with dignity
  - Promote a culture of inclusion and diversity

##### Fairness
- **Definition**: Making impartial and transparent decisions
- **In Action**:
  - Avoid favoritism and conflicts of interest
  - Make decisions based on merit and facts

##### Honesty
- **Definition**: Communicating truthfully and maintaining integrity
- **In Action**:
  - Provide accurate and truthful project updates
  - Do not misrepresent qualifications or project status

### Making Ethical Choices as a Project Manager

**Decision Framework**: When faced with ethical dilemmas, ask:
- Is it legal and in line with PMI standards?
- Does it respect all stakeholders?
- Is it fair and unbiased?
- Am I being completely honest?

**Key Principle**: "Do the right thing, even when no one is watching."

Upholding ethical standards builds credibility and long-term success.

## Project Charter

### Definition
A **Project Charter** is a formal contract between the Project Team and the Sponsor.

A Project Charter is an informal Contract that:
- Is entered into freely by two or more parties
- Cannot arbitrarily be changed
- Offers something of value for each party
- Is a living document that can evolve with changing conditions

### Four Purposes of a Charter
1. **Authorize the project manager to proceed**
2. **Help the project manager, sponsor, and team members** (if any are already assigned) develop a common understanding of the project
3. **Help the project manager, sponsor, and team members commit to the spirit of the project**
4. **Quickly screen out obviously poor projects**

### Typical Elements in a Project Charter

#### Core Elements
- **Title**
- **Scope Overview**
- **Business Case**
- **Background**
- **Milestone Schedule/Project Acceptance Criteria**
- **Risks, Assumptions, Constraints**
- **Resource Estimates**
- **Stakeholder List - initial**
- **Team Operating Principles**
- **Lessons Learned**
- **Signatures and Commitment**

#### Detailed Element Descriptions

##### Title
- A unique and meaningful project Title is critical
- The Title reflects key characteristics of a project (location, type, name of technology)

##### Scope Overview
- Used to help prevent scope creep
- Considered to be the project boundaries
- Quantifying the scope helps with understanding of project size

**Key Terms**:
- **Product Scope**: Characteristic features and functions of what is being created
- **Scope Creep**: Uncontrolled expansion to what was agreed upon

##### Business Case
- Project purpose or justification statement
- Answers the question "why?"
- Used to justify the necessity of the project
- Ties project to the organization's strategy
- Provides rationale or high-level cost/benefit estimates
- Persuades and inspires decision makers and team members

##### Background
- Used to provide more detail to support the scope statement and business case statements
- Background statement is optional

##### Milestone Schedule with Acceptance Criteria
- **Milestone Schedule**: Summary-level project schedule composed of major milestones and/or completion of deliverables
- **Acceptance Criteria**: Markers against which benefit/deliverables can be evaluated for completeness and correctness

##### Risks, Assumptions, and Constraints
- **Risk**: An uncertain situation which could have a negative or positive effect on the project if it occurs
- **Assumption**: Suppositions made during project planning that are treated as factual, though they've not been proven
- **Constraint**: Anything that limits project implementation

##### Resource Estimate
- A preliminary budget with level of confidence in the estimate
- Identify expenses the Project Manager can authorize
- Identify expenses the Sponsor needs to control

##### Stakeholder List
- Identify Stakeholders
- What does each Stakeholder care about?
- Who are the key Stakeholders?

##### Team Operating Principles
**Purpose**:
- Enhance team functioning
- Increase team effectiveness
- Ensure all parties are aware of what is expected

**Examples**:
- How to conduct meetings
- How to treat each other with respect
- How to accomplish work
- How to make decisions

##### Lessons Learned
**Definition**: Knowledge gained from one project which may be applicable to similar future projects

##### Signatures and Commitment
- Who is involved?
- Extent to which each person can make decisions
- Expected time commitment for each person
- The Project Sponsor, Project Manager, and business, customer, core team members show commitment by signing the Charter

### Agile Terms Used in Chartering Projects

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Commit to Work** | Team promises how much they will accomplish in timebox |
| **Timebox** | A defined number of hours or days to complete an agreed-upon amount of work |
| **Product Vision** | Description of customer satisfaction and alignment with business objectives at an initially high level |
| **Sprint (aka iteration)** | Short time period when team commits to creating specific deliverables |
| **Defer Decisions** | By making decisions as late as possible, teams have better information |
| **Sustainable Pace** | Amount of work team can consistently produce well |
| **Commitment** | Team follows through, only taking on tasks they can do |
| **Definition of Done** | Agreement on exactly how the Product Owner (PO) will judge the deliverables produced |
| **Share Knowledge** | Deliberatively capture and share tacit knowledge |

### Agile vs Plan-Driven Approach

#### Agile Charter Characteristics
- Agile projects adopt an informal approach rather than a formal project Charter
- An Agile Charter, after the first milestone, is a general roadmap of the product with epics depicting the benefits and project acceptance criteria
- One key idea of Agile projects is to make decisions at the latest responsible moment so that more complete information can be used in the decision
- Agile project begins with approval by the product owner for the team to start the project

#### Comparison Table

| Project Chartering Questions | Plan-Driven | Agile |
|------------------------------|-------------|-------|
| How do people initiate a project? | (Formal) Charter | (Informal) Charter |
| What are the purposes of a charter? | authorize, understand, commit, screen | authorize, understand, commit, screen |
| Why do the project? | business case | Business case / vision statement |
| What is included? | scope overview | product roadmap |
| When will it be done? | milestone schedule | Milestones as releases and iterations |
| How will outputs be judged? | success criteria at milestones | Acceptance criteria and definition of done |
| What might interfere? | risks, assumptions, constraints | Impediments – blockers, risks, assumptions |
| Who cares? | stakeholders | stakeholders |
| How will work be accomplished? | team operating principles and lessons learned | team operating principles and retrospectives |
| Pledge | signatures and commitment by sponsor and team | signatures and commitment by team |

### Directing and Managing Project Work

#### Key Characteristics
- Involves managing and performing the work described in the project management plan
- Majority of time and money is usually spent on execution
- The application area of the project directly affects project execution
- Products of the project are produced during the execution phase

#### Critical Success Factors
- The project manager needs to focus on leading the project team and managing stakeholder relationships to execute the project management plan successfully
- Project resource management, communications management, and stakeholder management are crucial to a project's success

### Providing Strong Leadership and a Supportive Culture

#### Project Manager Responsibilities
- Project managers must lead by example
- Demonstrate the importance of creating and then following good project plans and following them in project execution

#### Organizational Support
- Organizational culture can help project execution by:
  - Providing guidelines and templates
  - Tracking performance based on plans

### Monitoring and Controlling Project Work

#### Key Principles
- Changes are inevitable on most projects, so it's important to develop and follow a process to monitor and control changes
- Monitoring project work includes collecting, measuring, and disseminating performance information
- The project management plan provides the baseline for identifying and controlling project changes

#### Baseline Definition
**Baseline**: A starting point, a measurement, or an observation that is documented so that it can be used for future comparison

### Performing Integrated Change Control

#### Main Objectives
- Influencing the factors that create changes to ensure that changes are beneficial
- Determining that a change has occurred
- Managing actual changes as they occur

#### Change Control Evolution

##### Former View
- The project team should strive to do exactly what was planned on time and within budget
- **Problem**: Project teams could rarely meet original project goals

##### Modern View
- Project management is a process of constant communication and negotiation
- **Solution**: Changes are often beneficial, and the project team should plan for them

#### Integrated Change Control Process
1. **Create a project management plan**
2. **Create a log of the change request**
3. **Analyze the impact of the change**
4. **Decide on a course of action**
5. **Communicate the decision**
6. **Update the project management plan**

### Change Control System

#### Definition
Formal, documented process that describes when and how official project documents and work may be changed

#### Key Components
- Describes who is authorized to make changes
- Paperwork required for these changes
- Any automated or manual tracking systems the project will use

#### Change Control Board (CCB)
- **Definition**: A formal group of people responsible for approving or rejecting changes on a project
- **Functions**:
  - Provide guidelines for preparing change requests
  - Evaluate change requests
  - Manage the implementation of approved changes
- **Considerations**:
  - Some CCBs only meet occasionally, so it may take too long for changes to occur
  - Some organizations have policies in place for time-sensitive changes

#### Configuration Management
- **Definition**: Ensures that the descriptions of the project's products are correct and complete
- **Activities**:
  - Identifying and controlling the functional and physical design characteristics of products and their support documentation
  - Configuration management specialists identify and document configuration requirements
  - Control changes, record and report changes
  - Audit the products to verify conformance to requirements
