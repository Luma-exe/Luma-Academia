## Part 1 — Scenarios

### What is a Scenario?

- A scenario is a **short, action-driven written story** about your Persona
- They are **narratives of Personas achieving goals and satisfying needs**
- If a Persona is a representation of your users, then scenarios are the *stories* about that Persona
- A Persona acts, experiences, or lives through the design problem
- A Persona uses a "solution" to overcome aspects of the design problem
- A Persona may interact with others to use the solution together

### Why Use Scenarios?

Scenarios put the designer in the shoes of the user. As a design artefact, they allow the team to:

- Identify issues and concerns
- Identify tasks and task flows
- Illustrate areas to investigate further
- Illustrate design flow
- Understand the experience of end users
- Review, evaluate, and validate designs (during prototyping, testing, and implementation)

### Key Properties of Scenarios

- **Not** definite or absolute stories — they are concrete examples but remain rough, open, and flexible to change
- Written **in third person**, in words, without going into too much detail
- Convey a **general idea** of context and how the proposed design resolves the problem
- Do **not** include technical aspects or focus on specifics of a technology
- Scenarios should emphasise:
  - The **context** of use
  - **Usability and UX goals**
  - The **tasks** the user is performing

### When to Use Scenarios in the Design Process

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| User Research → Defining | Persona creation |
| Defining | Scenario writing |
| Ideation → Prototyping | Scenario refinement + revision |
| Evaluation | Persona-based scenario walk-throughs |

Scenarios serve three purposes across the process:
1. **To depict the current situation** (Problem Scenarios)
2. **To imagine new situations** (Future Use Scenarios)
3. **To test the new situations** (Scenario walk-throughs during evaluation)

---

### Types of Scenarios

#### Problem Scenario (Current State Scenario)
- Highlights **typical and critical situations** of use as they exist *now*
- Describes the pain points, frustrations, and barriers the Persona faces
- Drawn from your **problem statement**
- Written without a solution — focuses purely on the issue

#### Future Use Scenario (Future Scenario)
- Describes how the Persona uses the **proposed design solution** to achieve their goals
- Can contain:
  - **Activity scenarios** — what the user does
  - **Information scenarios** — what the user needs to know
  - **Interaction scenarios** — how the user interacts with the system
- Drawn from your **conceptual proposal**
- The process of going from problem → future scenario → prototype is **iterative**

### Worked Example — Jun (Bridge card game)

**Problem Statement:**
> "I want to be able to play Bridge (a card game) with my friends, but due to my age I am no longer eligible for a car licence, so I'm unable to drive and meet up with them."

**Conceptual Proposal:**
> A digital system that combines an interactive table, cameras, and special cards, allowing the user to play Bridge with their friends remotely, but without losing the intimacy that comes with seeing them in person.

**Current (Problem) Scenario:**
> Jun is a generally healthy elder who has recently been diagnosed with glaucoma. This means he can no longer drive at night, which was the time he used to go to his friend's house to play Bridge on Tuesdays. He now spends his nights at home, bored and frustrated. Most importantly, he misses hanging out with his friends and is starting to feel very isolated.

**Future Use Scenario:**
> Due to being unable to drive at night, Jun has missed his Bridge sessions for several months now. His friend Chen found out about this distributed interactive card system through which they can all meet remotely and play. Using an interactive table, projectors, and smart cards, Jun, Chen, and the rest of the group are now meeting remotely every Tuesday night just like they used to. Jun is back to enjoying friendship and not feeling isolated anymore.

### Worked Example — Tamira (Phone/Connectivity)

**Persona Bio:**
> Tamira Anand, 65 y/o, widow, 1 adult son (Rohan), retired nurse. Lives alone in a retirement village in Port Macquarie. Manages slight high blood pressure. Uses a smartphone mostly for FaceTime and voice messages. Tends to forget to charge her phone or where she's left it.

**Problem Scenario:**
> Tamira is at home in Port Macquarie and feels lonely. She misses her husband, who passed away 2 years ago, and her son and grandchildren, who live in Sydney. She decides to use FaceTime to call her grandchildren. But Tamira can't find the phone. She can't remember where she last left it. She tried to call it using her landline, but still can't hear anything. She thinks either the phone is dead or that it must be somewhere well hidden.

**Future Use Scenario:**
> Tamira wants to call her grandchildren using FaceTime, but she can't find the phone. Tamira uses this 'technology' to locate her phone. Because the technology is 'tethered' to her phone, it can help her find it anytime. The 'technology' will also alert her if her phone battery is running low so she can charge it. And if the phone rings and Tamira is not nearby, the 'technology' will also alert her through a series of devices distributed through the house. Because the technology is always at home and charges itself, Tamira will always have access to it.

### How to Write a Scenario — Step-by-Step

1. Choose **one Persona** and focus on them (you may refer to others joining them if suitable)
2. Identify **what is most important to support** — problems, situations, needs, goals
3. **Brainstorm** ways your conceptual design solution might help
4. Always consider the Persona — would this suit them? Would they want this?
5. **Start writing** the scenario

### Tips for Scenario Writing

- Start with bullet points but aim for a **narrative paragraph** — stories give more information than lists
- Don't over-polish — this is not literature; it's a rough, working document
- Write the way **people actually act** (which is not always rational)
- Maintain **character consistency** — keep your Persona in mind throughout
- Have the user interact with **other users** when appropriate
- Your Future Use Scenario illustrates your **design ideas in action** — refine it as you progress
- Don't worry too much about **technological limitations** at this stage — polish feasibility later
- Write **different scenarios** for how the design might act in different contexts
- GenAI (UTS 365 Copilot only if using interviewee data) can help with initial drafts

## Part 2 — Storyboards

### What is a Storyboard?

- If scenarios are **textual** representations of user experiences, storyboards are the **pictures** that tell those stories
- Storyboards are **visual narratives** of Personas achieving goals and satisfying needs
- They depict a **series of illustrations (shots/scenes)** showing actions, emotions, and thoughts users go through while interacting with a product
- Storyboards help designers **honour the real experiences** of the people for whom they are designing
