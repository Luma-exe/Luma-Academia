## Part 1: Digital Prototyping

### Context: Where Prototyping Fits in AT3

The AT3 assignment follows the standard **Design Thinking** process:

$$\text{Empathize} \rightarrow \text{Define} \rightarrow \text{Ideate} \rightarrow \underbrace{\text{Prototype} \rightarrow \text{Test}}_{\text{Week 10 focus}}$$

- The **Prototype** and **Test** stages are the current focus for AT3
- These stages are iterative — you prototype, test, refine, and repeat

---

### The Prototyping Spectrum: Lo-Fi to Hi-Fi

Prototypes exist on a fidelity continuum. As fidelity increases, speed decreases.

```
ROUGH but FAST ←————————————————————→ DETAILED but SLOW

[Sketching] → [Paper Prototyping] → [Hybrid/Mid-Fi] → [High-Fi] → [Near-Real]
```

|Stage|Tools|Characteristics|
|---|---|---|
|**Sketching**|Pen & paper|Roughest, fastest, cheapest|
|**Paper Prototyping**|Cut paper, post-its|Test user flows without screens|
|**Hybrid / Mid-Fi**|PowerPoint, Figma, Canva, Balsamiq, Marvel|Bridge ideas to interactive experience|
|**High Fidelity**|Sketch App, Framer AI|Close to real visual design|
|**Near-Real / Dev**|ProtoPie, InVision, Wizard of Oz, early builds|Highly realistic interaction|

> **Key principle:** The degree of exactness **increases** as you move from Lo-Fi → Hi-Fi. Start rough and cheap to fail fast, then invest detail only once direction is validated.

---

### Iterative Prototyping Process

The prototyping cycle is **not linear** — it loops:

$$\text{Prototype} \rightarrow \text{Test & Evaluate} \rightarrow \text{Refine} \rightarrow \text{Prototype} \rightarrow \text{Repeat}$$

- After sketching, **paper prototypes** test how users interact step-by-step toward a goal
- Next, extend to a **medium closer to real** (matching expected format and device)
- Hybrid/mid-fidelity tools bridge initial ideas with close-to-real interactive experiences

### Digital Prototyping Tools

#### Figma

- **Type:** Hybrid/mid-fi → near hi-fi
- **Use for:** UI design, clickable prototypes, transitions, team collaboration
- **Access:** Free at [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/) — also available via Canvas Modules → Prototyping Software and Templates
- **Key feature:** Prototyping & Transitions — link frames to simulate screen flows
- **Learn:** Official Figma video on Prototyping & Transitions (linked in slides)

#### InVision

- **Type:** Hi-fi prototyping and collaboration
- **Use for:** Linking static screens into interactive prototypes, sharing with stakeholders
- **Learn:** LinkedIn Learning intro course (linked in slides)

#### Balsamiq

- **Type:** Lo-fi / wireframing
- **Use for:** Rapid wireframe mockups with intentionally rough, sketch-like aesthetic — discourages over-attachment to visual design early on
- **Learn:** LinkedIn Learning video (linked in slides)

#### Sketch App

- **Type:** Hi-fi UI design (macOS only)
- **Use for:** Detailed visual design with reusable symbols/components
- **Learn:** Sketch's own YouTube Playlists (linked in slides)

#### Canva

- **Type:** Hybrid / accessible mid-fi
- **Access:** Education version available at [canva.com](https://www.canva.com/)
- **Use for:** Quick visual mockups with templates

#### Other Tools Mentioned

- **Marvel** — mid-fi, easy linking of screens
- **Framer AI** — hi-fi with AI-assisted design
- **ProtoPie** — hi-fi, complex interaction prototyping
- **Wizard of Oz** — a _technique_ (not a tool), where a human operator simulates system responses behind the scenes during testing

### Hardware / Physical Prototyping Tools

These tools are for **tangible interaction design** — prototyping physical products and environments.

#### Arduino

- Microcontroller platform for building interactive physical objects
- Programmable via C/C++-based Arduino IDE
- Website: [arduino.cc](https://www.arduino.cc/)
- UTS FEIT provides free/discounted software download

#### Raspberry Pi

- Full single-board computer (Linux-based)
- Quad Core 1.2GHz Broadcom CPU, 40-pin GPIO, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth 4.1
- Use for: more complex embedded systems, running full OS environments
- Website: [raspberrypi.org](https://www.raspberrypi.org/)

#### Makey Makey

- Invention kit that turns everyday conductive objects (e.g., bananas, foil) into keyboard/mouse inputs
- Great for low-barrier physical prototyping without programming
- Kits available at **UTS Library**
- Website: [makeymakey.com](https://makeymakey.com/)

> **Instructor note:** Makey Makey was shown in an earlier lecture video — the "Banana Space Bar" demo

### Fundamentals vs. Advanced: Key Distinction

||Fundamentals (current)|Advanced (31777/32509)|
|---|---|---|
|**Focus**|**Usability**|**User Experience (UX)**|
|**Definition**|How effectively and efficiently users achieve goals in particular environments with ease and minimal frustration|A person's perceptions and responses resulting from the use or anticipated use of a product, system, or service|
|**Outcome**|Design useful and usable technologies|Design technologies optimised for the _user's experience_|

> **Advanced IxD also builds a personal portfolio** — essential for industry readiness and work experience placements.
