## Project Failure Statistics - "What Went Wrong?"

### Historical Context (1995 Standish Group CHAOS Study)
- Only **16.2% of IT projects were successful** in meeting Scope, Time, and Cost goals
- Over **31% of IT projects were canceled** before completion

### PricewaterhouseCoopers (PWC) Study
- Over **half of all projects fail**
- Only **2.5% of corporations consistently meet their targets** for scope, time, and cost goals for all types of project

### Recent Improvements (Standish Group Updates)
#### 2015 Statistics
- **29% of projects were successful**
- Success rates by project size:
  - Small projects: **62% successful**
  - Medium projects: **9% successful**
  - Moderate projects: **21% successful**
  - Large projects: **6% successful**
- **39% of agile projects were successful** compared to **11% of waterfall projects**

#### 2020 Report
- **31% of projects were deemed successful** (completed on time, within budget, with desired features)
- **50% of projects were categorized as challenged** (completed but with delays, budget overruns, or reduced scope)
- **19% of projects were considered failures** (cancelled before completion or delivered but never used)

## What Is a Project?

### Official Definition
**"A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result"** - PMBOK® Guide, Sixth Edition, 2017

### Key Distinctions
- **Projects**: Temporary with specific objectives
- **Operations**: Work done to sustain the business (ongoing)
- **Project End Conditions**: When objectives are reached OR the project is terminated

### Examples of IT Projects
- A team of students creates a smartphone application and sells it online
- A company develops a driverless car
- A government group develops a system to track child immunizations
- A global bank acquires other financial institutions and needs to consolidate systems and procedures

## Project Attributes

### Core Characteristics
- **Unique purpose**: Each project creates something distinct
- **Temporary nature**: Has defined start and end dates
- **Drives change and enables value creation**: Transforms current state
- **Progressive elaboration**: Developed incrementally with increasing detail
- **Resource requirements**: Often from various areas and departments
- **Primary customer or sponsor**: Key stakeholder providing direction
- **Change driven**: Initiates organizational or process changes
- **Uncertainty and risk**: Inherent unknown factors

### Key Relationships
- **Project sponsor**: Provides direction and funding for the project
- **Project managers**: Work with sponsors, team members, and stakeholders to achieve goals

## Project Constraints

### The Triple Constraint
Projects must balance three primary constraints:
- **Scope**: What will be delivered
- **Time**: When it will be delivered  
- **Cost**: How much it will cost

*Note: These constraints are interdependent - changes to one typically affect the others*

## What is Project Management?

### Official Definition
**"The application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements"** - PMBOK® Guide, Sixth Edition, 2017

### Project Manager Objectives
- Strive to meet the **triple constraint** (Scope, Time, and Cost goals)
- Facilitate the entire process to meet needs and expectations of **project stakeholders**

## Project Stakeholders

### Definition
**Stakeholders are the people involved in or affected by project activities**

### Key Stakeholders Include
- The project sponsor
- Banks and other financial institutions
- The project manager  
- The project team
- Support staff
- Suppliers
- Opponents to the project

## Project Management Knowledge Areas

### Overview
Knowledge areas describe the **key competencies that project managers must develop**. Project managers must have knowledge and skills in **all 10 knowledge areas**.

### The 10 Knowledge Areas
1. **Scope Management**: Defining and controlling what is included in the project
2. **Schedule Management**: Planning and controlling project timelines
3. **Cost Management**: Planning, estimating, and controlling project costs
4. **Quality Management**: Ensuring project deliverables meet requirements
5. **Resource Management**: Acquiring and managing project team and resources
6. **Communications Management**: Ensuring timely and appropriate information flow
7. **Risk Management**: Identifying and responding to project risks
8. **Procurement Management**: Acquiring goods and services from outside vendors
9. **Stakeholder Management**: Identifying and managing stakeholder expectations
10. **Project Integration Management**: Coordinating all aspects of the project

## Project Management Tools and Techniques

### Planning and Scheduling Tools
- **MS Project**: Comprehensive project management software
- **JIRA**: Agile project management and issue tracking
- **Smartsheet**: Collaborative work management platform
- **Gantt charts**: Visual project timeline representation
- **Network diagrams**: Show task dependencies
- **Critical path analysis**: Identifies longest sequence of dependent tasks
- **Critical chain scheduling**: Resource-constrained scheduling method
- **Kanban board**: Visual workflow management tool

### Collaboration Tools
- **Teams**: Microsoft collaboration platform
- **SharePoint**: Document management and collaboration
- **Confluence**: Team collaboration and documentation
- **Slack**: Team communication platform

### Scope and Analysis Tools
- **Excel**: Spreadsheet analysis and planning
- **WBS (Work Breakdown Structure)**: Hierarchical task decomposition
- **CPM (Critical Path Method)**: Scheduling technique

### Cost and Prioritization Tools
- **PERT (Program Evaluation and Review Technique)**: Statistical scheduling method
- **MoSCoW**: Prioritization technique (Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have)
- **EVM (Earned Value Management)**: Performance measurement technique

### PMBOK® Guide Tool Categories
- **Data gathering**: Collecting project information
- **Data analysis**: Analyzing project data for decisions
- **Data representation**: Presenting information effectively
- **Decision making**: Making informed project choices
- **Communication**: Facilitating information exchange
- **Interpersonal and team skills**: Managing people and relationships
- **Ungrouped**: Other specialized techniques

## Program and Project Portfolio Management

### Programs

#### Definition
**"A group of related projects managed in a coordinated manner to obtain benefits and control not available from managing them individually"** - PMBOK® Guide, Sixth Edition, 2017

#### Examples in IT Field
- **Infrastructure programs**: Related infrastructure projects
- **Applications development programs**: Software development initiatives
- **User support programs**: End-user assistance initiatives

#### Program Manager Role
- Provides **leadership and direction** for project managers
- Coordinates projects within the program
- Ensures program-level benefits are realized

### Project Portfolio Management

#### Purpose
- Organizations group and manage **projects and programs as a portfolio** of investments
- Contributes to the **entire enterprise's success**
- **Portfolio managers** help organizations make wise investment decisions
- Select and analyze projects from a **strategic perspective**

### Organizational Project Management (OPM)

#### Definition
**Systematic management of projects, programs, and portfolios in alignment with the organization's strategic goals** - PMBOK® Guide, Sixth Edition, 2017

#### Goals of OPM
- **Align project outcomes** with strategic goals
- **Improve governance**, decision-making, and resource allocation
- **Increase project success rates**
- **Enable predictable and repeatable delivery** of value

## The Role of the Project Manager

### Key Responsibilities
- Work closely with **stakeholders**, especially sponsor and project team
- Must be familiar with **project management knowledge areas**
- Utilize various **tools and techniques** related to project management

### Job Description Elements
- **Planning and scheduling** project activities
- **Coordinating** project resources and activities
- **Working with people** to achieve project goals
- Project management skills needed in **every major IT field**:
  - Database administrator
  - Network specialist  
  - Technical writer

## PMI Talent Triangle® and Leadership Skills

### The Three Components
1. **Ways of Working** (previously Technical Project Management)
   - Technical project management skills
   - Project methodology knowledge
   
2. **Business Acumen** (previously Strategic and Business Management Skills)
   - Strategic and business management capabilities
   - Industry and organizational knowledge
   
3. **Power Skills** (previously Leadership Skills)
   - Leadership and interpersonal abilities
   - Communication and team management

### Leadership Styles
- **Laissez-faire**: Hands-off approach, team self-manages
- **Transactional**: Focus on exchanges and rewards/consequences
- **Servant leader**: Focus on serving and empowering team members
- **Transformational**: Inspire and motivate through vision and change
- **Charismatic**: Lead through personal charm and inspiration
- **Interactional**: Adapt leadership style based on situation and followers

## Top 10 IT Project Management Skills

1. **Agile & Scrum Project Management**: Agile frameworks like Scrum, Kanban, SAFe
2. **Technical Knowledge, AI in Project Management**: Understanding of IT concepts, including AI-driven project tracking and risk analysis tools
3. **Communication & Collaboration**: Clear technical and non-technical communication with stakeholders, team members, vendors, and clients
4. **Problem Solving & Critical Thinking**: Tackling technical and managerial challenges with logical, data-driven decision-making
5. **Project Planning & Scheduling**: Ability to define project scope, timelines, milestones, and use tools like MS Project, Jira, or Gantt charts for scheduling
6. **Leadership & Team Management**: Leading diverse technical teams, motivating them, resolving conflicts, and fostering collaboration
7. **Change Management & Adaptability**: Managing scope changes, evolving technologies, and shifting business needs while maintaining project focus
8. **Risk Management**: Identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks related to IT systems, security, budget, or integration failures
9. **Time Management & Prioritization**: Meeting deadlines and balancing competing priorities in fast-paced IT environments
10. **Green IT & Sustainability**: Leading eco-friendly IT initiatives

## Ethics in Project Management

### Definition
**Ethics** is loosely defined as **a set of principles that guide our decision making based on personal values** of what is "right" and "wrong"

### Professional Requirements
- Project managers often face **ethical dilemmas**
- **PMP® certification applicants** must agree to PMI's **Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct**
- **PMP® exam** includes several questions related to **professional responsibility**, including ethics

## Project Management Software

### Three Main Categories

#### Low-End Tools
- Handle **single or smaller projects** well
- Cost **under $100 per user**
- Suitable for basic project needs

#### Mid-Range Tools  
- Handle **multiple projects and users**
- Cost **$100-$400 per user**
- **Microsoft Project** is still the most popular in this category

#### High-End Tools
- Also called **enterprise project management software**
- Often **licensed on a per-user basis**
- **Microsoft Enterprise Project** is an example
- Suitable for large, complex organizational needs

### Additional Options
- **Several free or open-source tools** are also available
- **Hundreds of different products** available to assist in project management
