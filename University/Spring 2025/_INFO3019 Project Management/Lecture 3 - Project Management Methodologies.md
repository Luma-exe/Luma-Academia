## Project Life Cycle - Recap

### Four Main Phases
1. **Selecting and Initiating**
2. **Planning**
3. **Executing** (includes monitoring/Controlling)
4. **Closing and realizing**

### Phase Characteristics

#### Early Phases
- Resource needs are usually **lowest**
- Level of uncertainty (risk) is **highest**
- Project stakeholders have the **greatest opportunity to influence** the project

#### Middle Phases
- The certainty of completing a project **improves**
- **More resources** are needed

#### Final Phase
- Focus on ensuring that **project requirements were met**
- The **sponsor approves completion** of the project

## PMI's PMBOK® Guide

### PMI Certifications

### PMI Talent Triangle
Three key competency areas:
- **Ways of Working**
- **Business Acumen**
- **Power Skills**

### Five Project Management Process Groups

#### Process Group Definitions
1. **Initiating**: "Define a project or a new phase by obtaining authorization"

2. **Planning**: "Establish the project scope, refine objectives and define actions to attain objectives"

3. **Executing**: "Complete the work defined to satisfy project specifications"

4. **Monitoring and Controlling**: "Track, review, and regulate progress and performance, identify changes required, and initiate changes"

5. **Closing**: "Finalize all activities to formally close project of phase"

### PMBOK's 10 Knowledge Areas

#### Integration and Core Areas
1. **Integration Management**: "Processes and activities to identify, define, combine, unify, and coordinate the various processes and project management activities"

2. **Scope Management**: "Processes to ensure that the project includes all the work required, and only the work required, to complete the project successfully"

3. **Schedule Management**: "Processes to manage timely completion of the project"

4. **Cost Management**: "Processes involved in planning, estimating, budgeting, financing, funding, managing, and controlling costs so that the project can be completed within the approved budget"

5. **Quality Management**: "Processes and activities of the performing organization that determine quality policies, objectives, and responsibilities so that the project will satisfy the needs for which it was undertaken"

#### Resource and Communication Areas
6. **Resource Management**: "Processes that organize, manage, and lead the project team"

7. **Communications Management**: "Processes to ensure timely and appropriate planning, collection, creation, distribution, storage, retrieval, management, control, monitoring, and ultimate disposition of project information"

#### Risk and External Areas
8. **Risk Management**: "Processes of conducting risk management planning, identification, analysis, response planning, and control…to increase the likelihood and impact of positive events and decrease the likelihood and impact of negative events in the project"

9. **Procurement Management**: "Processes to purchase or acquire products, services, or results from outside the project team"

10. **Stakeholder Management**: "Processes to identify the people, groups, or organizations, that could impact or be impacted by the project, analyze their expectations and impact, and develop strategies for engaging them and managing conflicting interests"

## Agile Methodology

### The Agile Manifesto
Four core values:
1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan

### The Agile Principles

#### Customer and Development Focus
1. **Customer satisfaction** is given the highest priority
2. **Changes in requirements** are appreciated at any point in the project with a view to exploit competitive advantage for the customer
3. Present **working software** to the client frequently during the project execution phase
4. **People representing business and development teams** must work together constantly
5. Project team comprised of **motivated people** must be engaged in the project; support and trust must be extended to the project team
6. **Face-to-face communication** must be employed, which is considered effective and efficient. In other words, it is preferable to have a co-located project team

#### Process and Improvement Focus
7. **Project progress** is the progress made in developing working software
8. **Agile process** is meant to create sustainable development, which demands sponsors, developers, and users work at the same pace for a long period
9. Uninterrupted attention to **technical excellence and good design** boosts agility
10. **Simplicity**—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential
11. **Self-managed teams** help develop the best architectures, requirements, and designs
12. If not daily, the team routinely considers **how to improve effectiveness** and amends its behavior accordingly

### Agile Key Terms

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Agile (change-driven)** | A project method using iterative and continual processes, guided by an empowered mindset described in the Agile Manifesto |
| **Agile lifecycle** | Iteration-based production of solutions including inception, construction, and transition phases |
| **Iterative process** | A method to plan the entire project at only a high level at the start and plan portions to be done soon in detail, updating plans as more becomes known |
| **Product Owner** | The person who represents the client or customer and other stakeholders, prioritizing work and making timely decisions |
| **Scrum Master** | The person who facilitates and guides the team to improve performance, removing roadblocks |
| **Team Member** | A person who works on a project in cooperation with others to develop the solution |
| **Emergent Design** | Gradual understanding of the shape of a deliverable |

### Agile - Scrum Framework

#### Scrum Roles
1. **Product Owner**: 
   - Responsible for the business value of the project
   - Decides what work to do and in what order
   - Documents decisions in the product backlog

2. **Scrum Master**: 
   - Ensures that the team is productive
   - Facilitates the daily Scrum
   - Enables close cooperation across all roles and functions
   - Removes barriers that prevent the team from being effective

3. **Scrum Team/Development Team**: 
   - Cross-functional team of **5 to 9 people**
   - Organize themselves and the work
   - Produce desired results for each sprint
   - Sprint duration: normally **2 to 4 weeks**

#### Scrum Artifacts
**Definition**: An artifact is a useful object created by people

1. **Product Backlog**: List of features prioritized by business value

2. **Sprint Backlog**: Highest-priority items from the product backlog to be completed within a sprint

3. **Burndown Chart**: Shows the cumulative work remaining in a sprint on a day-by-day basis

#### Scrum Ceremonies
1. **Sprint Planning Session**: Meeting with the team to select a set of work from the product backlog to deliver during a sprint

2. **Daily Scrum**: Short meeting for the development team to share progress and challenges and plan work for the day

3. **Sprint Review**: Meeting in which the team demonstrates to the product owner what it has completed during the sprint

4. **Sprint Retrospective**: Meeting in which the team looks for ways to improve the product and the process based on a review of the actual performance of the development team

#### Scrum Activities by Process Group

| Process Group | Scrum Activity |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Initiating** | • Determine roles<br>• Decide how many sprints will compose each release and the scope of software to deliver |
| **Planning** | • Create product backlog<br>• Create sprint backlog<br>• Create release backlog<br>• Plan work each day in the daily Scrum<br>• Document stumbling blocks in a list |
| **Executing** | • Complete tasks each day during sprints<br>• Produce a shippable product at the end of each sprint |

## Agile SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework)

### Core Philosophy
**SAFe** = Combination of **Lean** and **Agile** principles

### Lean Principles
- **Waste elimination**
- **Process optimization**
- **Maximizing value with minimal resources**
- **Identify and remove non-value adding activities** from product development and delivery

### Agile Principles in SAFe
- Focus on **adaptability, collaboration** and **delivering working solutions frequently**
- **Individual and interactions**
- **Working software**
- **Customer collaboration**
- **Responding to change**

### SAFe Framework Structure
- Based on **5 disciplines**
- Generally practiced in **large organizations**
- Uses **Agile Release Train (ART)** when a product is too large to align multiple teams

### ART (Agile Release Train) Components

#### ART Roles (Beyond Standard Agile Roles)
Three critical leadership roles:

1. **Product Management**: 
   - Responsible for defining and supporting the build of MVP product that meets customer needs

2. **System Architect**: 
   - Small team or single person that defines the system's overall architecture

3. **Release Train Engineer**: 
   - A servant leader and ART coach
   - Facilitates ART events
   - Empowers and supports teams to deliver value

#### ART Events (Beyond Standard Agile Ceremonies)
Three critical ceremonies:

1. **PI Planning**: 
   - Regularly scheduled event bringing all teams together to align with shared goals

2. **System Demo**: 
   - A showcase at the end of each PI to demonstrate what has been achieved in the PI

3. **Inspect and Adapt**: 
   - ART level retrospective to evaluate current product
   - Reflect on processes and identify areas for improvement through a structured problem-solving workshop

## PRINCE2 Methodology

### Introduction to PRINCE2
- **Full Name**: PRojects IN Controlled Environments
- **Origin**: Developed by the **UK government**, now used globally
- **Usage**: Structured project management methodology widely used in government and private sectors
- **Focus**: Best practices, roles, processes and governance to ensure successful project execution

### PRINCE2 Project Structure
- A PRINCE2 project is **planned, monitored and controlled on a stage-by-stage basis**
- **Management Stage**: "The phase of the project that the Project Manager manages on behalf of the board at the end of which the board wishes to review progress, next stage plan, business case, risks to decide to continue with the project or not." (Axelos 2017)
- **Minimum Requirement**: A PRINCE2 project must have **minimum 2 management stages**

### PRINCE2 Certifications

#### Foundation Course
- **Level**: Entry-level certification
- **Focus**: Understanding the PRINCE2 methodology
- **Coverage**: Principles, themes, and processes

#### Practitioner Course
- **Purpose**: Apply the methodology in real project scenarios
- **Goal**: Confirm sufficient knowledge and understanding to apply and tailor the method in a range of different project environments and scenarios

### PRINCE2 Delivery Approaches
1. **Waterfall**: Delivery steps in sequence
2. **Agile**: Requirements, design, coding and testing all take place iteratively

### The 7 Principles of PRINCE2

1. **Continued Business Justification**: A project must have a valid reason throughout its lifecycle

2. **Learn from Experience**: Past lessons are used to improve future projects

3. **Defined Roles and Responsibilities**: Every team member knows their duties

4. **Manage by Stages**: Projects are divided into manageable phases

5. **Manage by Exception**: Only escalate issues when needed

6. **Focus on Products**: Deliverables are prioritized over activities

7. **Tailor to Suit the Project Environment**: Adapt PRINCE2 to fit different industries or organizations

### The 7 Themes of PRINCE2

1. **Business Case**: Why is the project needed?

2. **Organization**: Who is responsible for what?

3. **Quality**: What standards must be met?

4. **Plans**: How will tasks be carried out?

5. **Risk**: What could go wrong?

6. **Change**: How do we manage changes?

7. **Progress**: How do we track success?

### The 7 Processes of PRINCE2

1. **Starting Up a Project (SU)**: Initial planning and feasibility checks

2. **Directing a Project (DP)**: Senior management oversight

3. **Initiating a Project (IP)**: Defining the detailed project scope

4. **Controlling a Stage (CS)**: Managing and monitoring individual phases

5. **Managing Product Delivery (MP)**: Ensuring deliverables meet quality standards

6. **Managing a Stage Boundary (SB)**: Assessing progress before moving to the next stage

7. **Closing a Project (CP)**: Final review, lessons learned, and formal closure

### Benefits of PRINCE2 Certification
- **Globally Recognized**: Used by governments and businesses worldwide
- **Structured & Scalable**: Can be applied to projects of any size
- **Improves Career Prospects**: Many employers prefer PRINCE2-certified professionals
- **Focuses on Best Practices**: Ensures projects are well-structured and efficient
- **Adaptable**: Can be combined with Agile, Scrum, and other methodologies

### PRINCE2 Example Project: University Career Fair

#### Project Overview
- **Goal**: Successfully execute a university-wide career fair
- **Timeline**: 3 months
- **Budget**: $10,000
- **Stakeholders**: University Admin, Students, Employers, Event Coordinators

#### PRINCE2 Principles in Action
1. **Continued Business Justification**: Career fair must align with university goals
2. **Learn from Experience**: Use lessons from previous fairs
3. **Defined Roles & Responsibilities**: Assign clear roles for students, staff, and vendors
4. **Manage by Stages**: Break project into planning, execution, and review phases
5. **Manage by Exception**: Only escalate major issues to university admin
6. **Focus on Products**: Ensure key deliverables: venue, employers, student participation
7. **Tailor to Suit the Environment**: Adjust PRINCE2 methodology based on university culture

#### PRINCE2 Processes in Action
1. **Starting Up a Project**: Define project scope and get university approval
2. **Directing a Project**: Senior management provides strategic oversight
3. **Initiating a Project**: Create detailed project plan and assign responsibilities
4. **Controlling a Stage**: Monitor venue booking, employer invitations, logistics
5. **Managing Product Delivery**: Ensure booths, banners, schedules are ready
6. **Managing Stage Boundaries**: Assess progress before moving to event execution
7. **Closing a Project**: Gather feedback, document lessons learned, and report to stakeholders

### PRINCE2: Predictive vs Adaptive

#### PRINCE2 as Predictive (Plan-Driven) Methodology
**Primary Classification**: PRINCE2 is **primarily a predictive methodology**, but it also has adaptive elements

##### Key Predictive Aspects
- Detailed project plans created at the start
- Phased, sequential process with formal stage gates
- Heavy focus on governance, risk management, and documentation
- Best suited for projects with **clear requirements and fixed scope**
- **Detailed upfront planning** is essential

##### Problems with Predictive (Waterfall) Methodology
- Sequential approach can be inflexible
- Changes are difficult and expensive to implement
- Limited customer feedback until late in the project
- High risk if requirements change

#### PRINCE2's Adaptive (Agile) Capabilities

##### Key Adaptive Aspects
- The principle of **"Tailor to Suit the Environment"** allows PRINCE2 to be customized for Agile projects
- **Manage by Exception** approach allows teams to work with autonomy while senior management intervenes only when necessary
- **Focus on deliverables (products)** rather than strict adherence to processes
- Can be tailored to work in **iterative and flexible environments**

## PRINCE2 Agile

### What is PRINCE2 Agile?
- **Definition**: PRINCE2 Agile combines the **flexibility and responsiveness of Agile** with the **structured framework of PRINCE2**
- **Purpose**: Developed for projects that require flexibility but also benefit from the governance and control of PRINCE2

### Core Components of PRINCE2 Agile

#### PRINCE2 Principles
- **7 core principles** like focus on products, learn from experience, and manage by stages

#### Agile Framework Integration
- Incorporates **Agile frameworks** such as **Scrum** and **Kanban**

#### Tailoring Approach
- The PRINCE2 methodology is **tailored to allow the flexibility of Agile**, depending on the needs of the project

### PRINCE2 Agile Framework Elements

#### Governance
- PRINCE2 provides **project management governance**
- Ensures **clear roles, stages, and accountability**

#### Agile Practices
- **Scrum, Kanban, and Lean methodologies** are incorporated for flexibility and iterative delivery

#### Focus on Value Delivery
- Emphasis on **delivering value through incremental releases and collaboration**

### Roles in PRINCE2 Agile

1. **Agile Project Manager**: Manages the project using Agile practices within the PRINCE2 framework

2. **Team Members**: Cross-functional teams that collaborate and adapt to changing requirements

3. **Product Owner**: Ensures the product meets customer needs and communicates project progress

4. **Agile Coach**: Provides guidance on Agile techniques and facilitates Agile ceremonies

### Benefits and Challenges of PRINCE2 Agile

#### Benefits
- **Flexibility**
- **Better risk management**
- **Faster delivery**
- **Enhanced collaboration**
- **Customer-focused outcomes**

#### Challenges
- **Potential for confusion** between Agile and traditional PRINCE2 roles
- **Need for Agile expertise**
- **Resistance to change** in organizations
